# Area 3 (encounters): requirements

What to capture, as consolidated from earlier design conversations (one with Claude, one with
Gemini). This describes **what to capture, not how to model it**; modeling decisions and their
reasons are in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). Items marked unresolved or proposed are open
questions.

## Who is encountered

### 1. People

- Deceased family, by specific role: mother, father, son, daughter, brother, sister,
  grandmother, grandfather, great-grandparent, spouse or partner, aunt or uncle, cousin,
  in-law, step-relation, other extended relative.
- Living family (same roles).
- Deceased friends and acquaintances, with type where stated (childhood friend, military
  comrade, coworker, neighbor, mentor).
- Living friends and acquaintances.
- Ancestors or relatives the experiencer never met.
- Unrecognized humans who are never identified.
- Non-religious historical figures (Lincoln, Einstein) and deceased celebrities.

### 2. God and Christian figures

- God, recording the term used ("God," "the Source," "the Light," "the Universe," "Allah,"
  "Om").
- Jesus.
- The Holy Spirit.
- The Blessed Virgin Mary, as her own category (attributes below).
- Angels: unnamed, and named archangels (Michael, Gabriel) recorded by name.
- Saints, split by tradition, and NOT limited to Roman canonization:
  - Catholic and Orthodox saints (Padre Pio, St. Jude, St. Francis).
  - Protestant saints and luminaries (Johann Gerhard, Martin Luther, John Wesley, William
    Wilberforce, Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Charles Spurgeon). Without explicit instruction, an LLM
    will treat "saint" as Roman-canonized or file these people as secular historical figures.
  - Early church fathers (Augustine, Athanasius, Chrysostom, Polycarp).
  - Biblical figures (Moses, Elijah, Abraham, Paul, the apostles).

### 3. Figures from other traditions

- Muhammad.
- Shia Imams.
- The Buddha and bodhisattvas (Guanyin, Amitabha).
- Hindu deities (Krishna, Shiva, Vishnu, Ganesha).
- Yama, his messengers (Yamadutas), and Chitragupta, including the characteristic motifs:
  messengers with ropes, a ledger of deeds, and being sent back because of a clerical error
  ("wrong person").
- Guru Nanak and other Sikh figures.
- Joseph Smith and Moroni. These are LDS figures; I don't classify LDS as Christian.
- Baha'u'llah.
- Modern gurus and teachers (Yogananda, Sai Baba).
- Living religious leaders (one Iranian study found a living cleric in an NDE).
- Other named religious figures.

### 4. Unidentified beings

- Beings of light.
- A voice or felt presence with no visual form.
- Guides.
- Councils of elders and robed figures.
- Orbs.

### 5. Hostile beings

- The devil and demons.
- Iconography type, keeping cultural and scriptural imagery separate:
  - Folkloric: red skin, horns, pitchfork, cloven hooves. This is medieval and theatrical
    imagery, not biblical.
  - Scriptural: serpent, dragon, roaring lion, or a deceptive "angel of light"
    (2 Corinthians 11:14).
  - Shadow beings: dark, faceless silhouettes.
  - Mocking or grotesque human-like spirits.
  - Amorphous creatures: beasts, slime, non-humanoid monsters.
  - An unseen terrifying presence in darkness.

### 6. Animals

- Role: the experiencer's own pet; someone else's pet; a working or farm animal; animals as
  scenery (birds singing, deer grazing); an animal-form guide (an intelligent or speaking
  animal, a totem); a hostile beast.
- Species: dog, cat, horse, bird, rodent or other small pet (rats, hamsters, rabbits,
  ferrets), reptile or amphibian, other.

### 7. Control categories (expected to be rare)

- Extraterrestrials explicitly called aliens, Greys, or UFO occupants. Keep these separate from
  merely sci-fi settings (spacecraft, metal rooms, another planet) and from luminous beings the
  experiencer calls "interdimensional."
- Folkloric beings: fairies, elves, centaurs, vampires.
- Fictional characters: Gandalf, Sherlock Holmes, film characters.
- Mechanical or artificial beings.

## Attributes for every encounter

- Vital status versus what the experiencer knew. This gives a two-by-two grid:
  - Dead and known to be dead.
  - Dead but not known to be dead (Peak in Darien), with how the death was learned afterward,
    as reported.
  - Alive and known to be alive ("inverse Peak in Darien").
  - Unknown.
- Prior familiarity: familiar before the experience; unfamiliar before it; or an explicit claim
  of never having known of the figure (e.g., "I had never heard of Jesus").
- How and when identity was established:
  - Recognized on sight.
  - Announced or told during the experience ("I am Jesus").
  - Intuited during it ("I just knew").
  - Identified afterward from a photograph, family description, artwork, or reading.
  - Intuited afterward ("Later I felt in my heart it was...").
  - Never identified.
- Hedged or disavowed recognition ("someone who reminded me of...", "I know how this sounds").
- Representation:
  - Nominal or abstract: a formless light or presence given a name ("the light was Krishna,"
    "the light was God").
  - A generic humanoid figure (robes, light) without specific attributes.
  - Iconographically specific (wounds, elephant head, four arms, trident, halo, monastic
    habit).

  Record the specific visual details listed. Miller made this distinction for Hindu figures;
  it applies to every figure.
- The term used, separate from the referent. A Christian's "God" may mean Christ;
  Arabic-speaking Christians say "Allah."
- Where: observed at the bedside or elsewhere on earth during an out-of-body experience, versus
  in an otherworldly setting.
- For living people seen in an otherworldly setting: how the experiencer explains it ("higher
  self," "bilocation," "a dream element").
- For the deceased: time since death, and whether they appeared at the age they died. Note:
  one questionnaire version asks the age question directly.
- For step-relations and in-laws: whether the person was a formative presence (raised the
  experiencer, shared a household in childhood) and any attachment language ("like a second
  father," "barely knew her"). Only if stated. This allows a later grouping by functional
  closeness, but don't force it.
- Disposition: loving, neutral or instructive, hostile, or ambiguous and unsettling.
- For hostile beings: whether invoking a name ("Jesus," "God") dispelled them, and which name.
- Roles and actions:
  - Sends the experiencer back, or discusses returning.
  - Conveys an unfinished purpose.
  - Gives comfort or affection.
  - Offers generic moral teaching.
  - Christ-specific content (wounds or the cross, forgiveness of sins, worship, reference to
    the Father, judgment).
  - Pluralist or universalist statements.
  - Theological or prophetic revelation.

  These should cover King's (2024) six themes for Jesus encounters, plus the Christ-specific
  content his themes didn't foreground.
- Communication mode, if stated: spoken words, telepathic or mind-to-mind, or instantaneous
  knowing.
- The experiencer's response: awe, fear, awareness of sin, worship.
- Evidence: a verbatim quote, and whether it comes from the narrative or a questionnaire
  answer. (Verbatim text stays in the private data repository; see DECISIONS.md.)

## Figure-specific details

- The Blessed Virgin Mary has three specificity levels:
  - A generic maternal presence (a radiant woman, unnamed).
  - Named as Mary or the mother of Jesus, without confessional content.
  - Confessionally Catholic content: the rosary, the scapular, purgatory, novenas,
    intercession, apparition titles (Guadalupe, Fatima).

  Also record any instructions she gives (verbatim; in the private data repository only). Mary
  is honored in Islam, so her presence alone isn't a mismatch for Muslim experiencers;
  Catholic-specific content would be.
- Animals: species, the pet's name if given, whether it was the experiencer's own pet, whether
  it communicated (by speech or telepathically), and whether the experiencer knew it had died.

## Derived in analysis, not extracted

These depend on combining encounter data with background data, so the coder shouldn't judge
them:

- Whether a figure is from a tradition outside the experiencer's own (cross-tradition
  encounters).
- "Theological mismatch," such as Catholic-specific Marian content in a Protestant's account.
- Functional attachment groupings for family.
- Nested Jesus definitions (from "any mention" to "explicitly identified during the
  experience"). These use the separate all-mentions extraction.

## Decided against

- Denominational tropes that are too elastic to code: Orthodox aerial toll-houses,
  Swedenborgian spheres, Charismatic spiritual-warfare imagery. Many distressing NDEs could be
  read as any of these.
- The rescue-after-calling-on-Jesus arc as a media fingerprint (it's a natural emotional arc).
  Invoking a name to dispel a hostile being is still recorded as an encounter attribute above.
- Model self-reported confidence scores. Use disagreement between two models instead.

## Unresolved

- The Gemini draft was inconsistent: it filed Archangel Michael under Catholic saints and
  Moses, Elijah, and Metatron under "Judaic prophets." Proposed rule: angels are their own
  category regardless of tradition, and biblical figures are a shared category. Many
  Protestants, including Lutherans, commemorate St. Michael and All Angels, so filing Michael
  as Catholic would be wrong.
- Where a Protestant saint ends and a secular historical figure begins: Wilberforce, Lincoln,
  C.S. Lewis. A possible rule: if the person is known primarily for faith (theologian, martyr,
  missionary, commemorated on a church calendar), they count as a religious figure; otherwise
  they're a historical figure. Record the name either way.
- A being of light later named Jesus should be a single encounter with an identity and a
  timing, not two encounters.
- Hostile animals overlap with hostile beings; decide where they go.

## Proposed additions (not yet discussed)

- Children who were never born: miscarried, stillborn, or aborted children or siblings.
- People not yet born, such as the experiencer's future children.
- The experiencer's own "higher self" or double.
- Groups such as a welcoming crowd of relatives: decide whether these are counted as
  individuals or as one collective encounter.
- Added November 2026: clergy the experiencer knew personally, kept separate from historical
  saints and religious luminaries.
